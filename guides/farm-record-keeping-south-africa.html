Farm record keeping in South Africa: a weekly system for kampe, livestock and repairs

Farm record keeping on a small South African plaas often lives in a notebook, a few WhatsApp chats and someone's memory. That works until a buyer asks for herd numbers, a vet asks when a group was last treated, or a borehole pump fails for the second time in a month. This guide sets out the records worth keeping and a 20-minute weekly routine to keep them current.

The seven records that cover most small farms

  1. Kamp / field register: each kamp or land, its size in hectares, what is on it (crop, grazing, fallow) and when it was last rested or worked.
  2. Herd register: headcount by species and kamp, plus births, deaths, purchases and sales with dates. Note each animal's or group's identification mark.
  3. Treatment log: date, animal or group, the product name exactly as your vet or the label gives it, who gave it, and the withdrawal date from the label. Leave dose decisions to your vet.
  4. Grazing rotation: which group moved into which kamp on which day, and when it is due out.
  5. Repairs and infrastructure: fences, pumps, boreholes, troughs, vehicles and sheds, with open and closed dates for each job.
  6. Money in and out (ZAR): sales, feed, fuel, repairs and wages by month, so your bookkeeper gets one clean list.
  7. People: roster, hours, leave and pay dates for farm workers.

A 20-minute weekly routine

  1. Count and reconcile: update headcounts per kamp and write down any birth, death, sale or purchase from the week.
  2. Treatments: copy anything given this week into the treatment log and check which withdrawal dates end soon.
  3. Grazing: record moves and set the next move date for each group.
  4. Walk the list of open repairs: close what was fixed and add what broke.
  5. Money: add the week's slips and invoices while they are still in your bakkie.
  6. Next week: write the three jobs that must happen, with a day next to each.

Carrying capacity: the quick maths

Grazing demand is often compared in large stock units (LSU), a standard unit roughly equal to one mature head of cattle. Carrying capacity is usually given as hectares per LSU for your veld type and rainfall.

Example only: 120 ha of grazing at a rate of 5 ha per LSU = 120 ÷ 5 = 24 LSU. If your herd already adds up to 30 LSU, you are over that figure and need to rest kampe, buy feed or reduce numbers.

The right rate for your farm depends on veld condition, rainfall and season. Ask your local agricultural extension officer or your provincial Department of Agriculture for the figure for your area.

Rules worth checking (not legal advice)

  • Animal identification: the Animal Identification Act (Act 6 of 2002) requires owners of livestock such as cattle, sheep, goats and pigs to register an identification mark and mark their animals. Check the current requirements with the Department of Agriculture.
  • Farm workers: the Basic Conditions of Employment Act and Sectoral Determination 13 (farm worker sector) set rules on hours, leave and payslips. Confirm details with the Department of Employment and Labour.
  • Personal information: worker ID numbers and contact details fall under POPIA. Keep them out of group chats and store only what you need.

Notebook, spreadsheet or app?

A notebook is quick in the veld but hard to total. A spreadsheet totals well but is awkward on a phone in the kraal. A phone app works if it opens fast, works with weak signal and does not ask you to retype the same list every week. Pick the one you will actually open every Friday.

FAQ

What records should a small farm in South Africa keep?

Most small farms are covered by a kamp register, herd register, treatment log, grazing rotation, repair list, money in and out, and worker hours and leave. Some records, like animal identification marks and worker payslips, are also legal requirements, so confirm the details with the relevant department.

How do I work out carrying capacity?

Divide your grazing hectares by the hectares-per-LSU rate for your veld. For example, 120 ha at 5 ha per LSU gives 24 LSU. Get the correct rate for your area from an agricultural extension officer.

Does Farm Desk tell me which vaccine or dose to use?

No. Farm Desk only reminds you when something is due and logs what you gave. Product and dose decisions stay with your veterinarian.
